nf-demultiplex-matfet · overview for review

Mother or fetus?

A pregnancy tissue sample contains cells from two people. This pipeline works out, cell by cell, which come from the mother and which from the fetus, and says when the data are not good enough to tell.

At a glance

What it does
Labels every cell of a pregnancy single-cell RNA-seq library as maternal, fetal or unknown.
How
Pools all libraries from one pregnancy, finds the two people's genotypes from the DNA variants in the reads, checks that a second person is really there, and uses marker genes to decide which one is the fetus.
Does it work
On 57 test runs with a known answer, it made the right call every time (one very small fetus was reported as too small to use). On real data, its fetal fractions follow the tissue biology, which it never sees.
Result so far
REQ-68805, 27 pregnancies: a fetus identified in 19 (10 male with high confidence, 9 likely female, provisional); no second person in 7; one probable sample mix-up.
Main caveats
No independent ground truth yet; female-fetus calls are provisional; a second person is not always the fetus.

Part 1
How it works
The problem with the first version, the approach that replaced it, and the two decisions at its centre.
1

The problem

In shortAnalysing each library on its own invents fetal cells: the genotyping tool always splits cells into two groups, even when only the mother is present.

Each cell's RNA reads carry its DNA variants at thousands of common SNPs, so cells can be grouped by the person they came from. The standard tool for this, Souporcell, is told how many people to expect (two) and always returns that many groups. The first version of the pipeline ran it on each library separately. In a library with few or no fetal cells it simply cut the mother's cells in half, which looks just like a confident maternal/fetal split. On REQ-68805 that gave one usable library out of nine.

2

The approach

In shortPool every library from one pregnancy, find the two genotypes once, check that a second person is really there, then use marker genes to say which one is the fetus.

Libraries from the same pregnancy share the same mother and fetus. Pooling them into one pregnancy group means a fetus that is rare in most libraries but common in one is still found, and every library is then assigned against the same two genotypes. The pipeline has six steps; steps 4 and 5 are the two decisions explained next.

  1. each library
    Count alleles
    Re-align reads and record which version of each SNP every cell shows.
  2. each pregnancy
    Pool libraries
    Merge all libraries' cells, remembering where each came from.
  3. each pregnancy
    Find two genotypes
    Souporcell splits all cells into two groups and builds a genotype profile for each.
  4. each pregnancy
    Is there a second person?
    Reject the split if the two groups are halves of one person.
    Section 3 →
  5. each pregnancy
    Which one is the fetus?
    Marker genes decide; otherwise no label.
    Section 4 →
  6. each pregnancy
    Split back and report
    A label for every cell of every library, and one report per pregnancy.

Two rules apply throughout. The pipeline never forces an answer: when it cannot tell, cells are labelled unresolved, never "maternal" by default. And genotype only says that two people are present; which one is the fetus always comes from gene expression.

3

Is there a second person?

In shortCompare the two groups' genotype profiles. A mother and fetus disagree at roughly half of the SNPs both can be read at; two halves of one person disagree far less. The cut-off is 0.40.

For each of its two groups, Souporcell writes a genotype profile. A mother and her fetus share one copy of every SNP, but the fetus's other copy comes from the father, so their profiles still differ at about half of the informative SNPs. Two halves of the same person should be identical, apart from errors where too few reads were available. The pipeline measures the discordant fraction: SNPs where the two profiles disagree, divided by SNPs called in both.

The result is one of three verdicts for the pregnancy:

VerdictWhenWhat happens
✓two_profilesDiscordant fraction ≥ 0.40 and the smaller group has ≥ 20 singletsTwo people. Go on to decide which is the fetus.
–unresolvedDiscordant fraction below 0.40, or no SNP readable in both profilesOne person. No cell is labelled maternal or fetal.
◐one_profileProfiles differ, but the smaller group has under 20 singletsPossibly a second person, too few cells to use. No labels.

How the 0.40 cut-off was chosen, and how well it separates real from fake splits, is shown in section 5.

4

Which one is the fetus?

In shortIf one group expresses the Y-chromosome gene RPS4Y1, it is a male fetus (high confidence). Otherwise placental marker genes vote (provisional, likely female). If neither decides, no label is given.

Marker expression is pooled over all of the pregnancy's libraries and compared between the two groups.

StepRuleResult
Tier 1RPS4Y1 in at least 10% of one group's cells and 5 times more than in the other. A mother has no Y chromosome, so this can only be a male fetus.Fetal, male, high confidence; the other group maternal.
Tier 2Placental trophoblast markers (HLA-G, XAGE2, XAGE3, HAPLN1, PITX2, HPGDS, GAPLINC) vote for the group where they are enriched; a clear majority decides.Fetal, likely female, provisional; the other group maternal.
OtherwiseNeither tier decides.Both groups unresolved.

The absence of RPS4Y1 is never taken as evidence that a group is maternal: the fetus may be female. Tier 2 calls are consistent so far but have not been independently validated, which is why they are provisional. Placental markers are only expressed by trophoblast, so a female fetus in tissue without trophoblast stays unresolved.

Technical detail: thresholds

Defaults in nextflow.config: rps4y1_fraction_floor = 0.10, rps4y1_min_fold = 5, min_vote_margin = 2, min_marker_detected_fraction = 0.10, min_marker_fraction_delta = 0.05, min_marker_logfc = 1.0, min_cluster_cells = 50 (a group under 50 singlets downgrades a high-confidence call to provisional). Only singlets count; doublets and unassigned cells are never labelled.

Part 2
Does it work?
First on data where the answer is known, then on real pregnancies, where the biology provides an independent check.
5

Tests with a known answer

In shortOn 57 test runs with a known answer, every fake split fell below 0.40 and every real fetus above it. Fetal cells are found down to about 1% of cells, and below that the pipeline reports "too small" rather than guessing.

The spike-in experiment

Real data never tells us the answer, so the method was first tested on data where we know it. Each spike-in test mixes about 7,000 maternal cells of known origin (from CW5) with a chosen number of known fetal cells, from none up to 10%, twice per level.

Does the gate separate real from fake?

What to expect on new data

  • Clear cases read below about 0.35 or above about 0.45. Between those, check the marker evidence and how the two genotypes are spread over libraries before relying on the call: on real data the margin either side of 0.40 was only 0.04.
  • Detection limit: about 35–70 fetal cells in a 7,000-cell pregnancy (0.5–1%). With 17 fetal cells the result was one_profile, "too small", not a wrong call.
  • Cell-level accuracy once a fetus is accepted: from 1% upwards, 97–100% of fetal cells found and 89–100% of fetal calls correct.
How the gate was designed
  1. Requirement. Reject two halves of one person, using only Souporcell's own outputs.
  2. Candidates, each measured on the same test runs:
    SignalResultKept?
    Split-half replication of separating SNPs0.75–0.93 at every fetal fraction, including noneNo
    Number of separating SNPsGrows with depth: a fake CW4 split had as many as a real 1% fetusNo
    Ambient RNA fitted by SouporcellPerfect on the test runs; failed on real data (below)Replaced
    Discordant fractionSeparated every runYes
  3. Cut-off. 0.40 lies in the gap between the highest fake (0.30) and lowest real (0.53) test run, chosen from test runs only.
  4. Check, not tune. Real pregnancies were then used to check the cut-off, never to move it.
  5. Minimum size. At least 20 singlets in the smaller group: a profile built from very few cells makes real fetal cells look like doublets.

Why not ambient RNA?

The first version of the gate used Souporcell's estimate of ambient RNA, which reads very high when the two groups are one person. It separated the test runs perfectly, all from one tissue and chemistry, but not real data from other tissues and chemistries.

Part 3
Using the results
What a user gets, how to read it, and where the pipeline's answers stop.
7

What you get

In shortOne table per library with a label for every cell, and one report per pregnancy saying how far to trust it. Read the report first, then take the cells.

reports/<pregnancy>.html gives the verdict, which group is fetal and why, any warnings, and cells per library. <library>/cell_assignments.tsv.gz has one row per CellRanger cell barcode, so it joins directly onto that library's AnnData or Seurat object. Its label column is the answer:

LabelMeaningUse as
fetal_high_confidence_maleFetal; fetus identified by the Y chromosomeFetal. The strongest call.
fetal_marker_supportedFetal; identified by placental markers, likely femaleFetal, provisional.
maternal_likelyThe other person, once the fetus is identifiedMaternal.
unresolvedThe pregnancy could not be labelledUnknown. Not maternal.
doubletTwo cells in one dropletExclude.
unassignedToo few informative readsExclude.

A library can have no fetal cells even when its pregnancy has a fetus: the fetal cells may all be in another library of the pregnancy. All other output files are evidence and audit trail; they are listed in appendix B.

8

Limits

In shortValidated on synthetic tests from one pregnancy and checked against biology, not against independently genotyped mothers and fetuses.
  • A second person is not always the fetus. The gate asks whether two people are present. A library from another donor would pass like a fetus (D6). Checking the per-library pattern against the tissue helps catch this.
  • No independent ground truth. The strongest available test would be genotyping mother and fetus directly, or published per-cell annotations.
  • Female fetuses depend on placental markers; in tissue without trophoblast they stay unresolved.
  • Very small fetal fractions, below about 0.5% of a 7,000-cell pregnancy, are not detected.
  • Large pregnancies (tens of libraries) have not been run; computing resources are not yet sized for them.
  • The reverse question, finding rare maternal cells in fetal tissue, needs a different method; this pipeline is designed to find fetal cells in maternal tissue.
  • Not supported yet: maternal reference samples, and pools of more than one pregnancy.

Running it

One CSV row per library; libraries sharing a group_id are treated as one pregnancy.

module load cellgen/nextflow
nextflow run nf-demultiplex-matfet/main.nf \
    --input samplesheet.csv \
    --outdir results \
    -profile sanger \

All rows are checked before any job starts. Step 1 takes 2.4–11.4 hours per library (median 5.6), five at a time; the pregnancy-level steps follow within minutes to an hour. -resume reuses finished work.

groups/<pregnancy>/cluster_marker_summary.tsv and gene_resolution.tsv: marker evidence

Per genotype group and marker, pooled over the pregnancy's libraries, singlets only: n_cluster_cells, n_pos and frac_pos (cells expressing it), cp10k (expression per 10,000 counts) and mean_counts. gene_resolution.tsv records how each marker gene was found in the expression matrix; the run stops if one is missing.

groups/<pregnancy>/discovery/: Souporcell's genotype groups

clusters.tsv: Souporcell's call for every cell (barcodes written <library>::<barcode>), with probabilities; all labels are built from it. cluster_genotypes.vcf: the two genotype profiles compared by the gate (samples 0 and 1; GT is the call, ./. none, AO/RO supporting reads). ambient_rna.txt: quality metric. clusters_tmp.tsv and *.err: intermediate output and logs.

groups/<pregnancy>/matrix/: audit trail of the pooling

barcode_provenance.tsv maps every pooled barcode to its library and CellRanger barcode; library_offsets.tsv and matrix_qc.tsv give each library's place and size in the pooled matrix; integrity_check.txt lists the 19 checks that pooling lost nothing (any failure stops the run); manifest.json records inputs and parameters.

souporcell/<library>/ and pipeline_info/

souporcell/ holds step 1's allele counts (reusable via souporcell_dir), the re-aligned reads (about 10 GB per library), and Souporcell's own per-library analysis. That per-library analysis is exactly what produced fake splits (section 1): do not use it as a result. pipeline_info/ has Nextflow's report, timeline, task table and workflow graph.

C

Glossary

Allele
One version of a DNA position; everyone carries two, one from each parent.
Ambient RNA
Free-floating RNA that ends up in droplets alongside a cell's own RNA.
Discordant fraction
Share of SNPs, readable in both genotype profiles, where the profiles disagree. Mother–fetus about 0.5; one person split in two well below 0.40.
Doublet
A droplet that captured two cells; excluded.
Genotype
The set of DNA variants a person carries.
Genotype profile
The genotype inferred for one group of cells: 0, 1 or 2 copies of the alternative version at each SNP.
Pregnancy group
All libraries from one pregnancy (one mother, one fetus); the samplesheet's group_id.
RPS4Y1
A gene on the Y chromosome; its expression marks male (fetal) cells.
Singlet
A droplet with exactly one cell.
SNP
A DNA position where people commonly differ.
Souporcell
The tool that groups cells by genotype.
Spike-in test
A synthetic sample with a known number of cells of known origin mixed in.
Trophoblast
Fetal cells of the placenta; the source of the placental markers.
